## 🔗 Why the Same Vehicle Appears Again After Several Timesteps

- **Each vehicle is tracked continuously across time.**  
  The simulation doesn’t just log a vehicle once — it logs its state at *every timestep* until it leaves the network.  

- **Timesteps are snapshots.**  
  At timestep 1, you see `easyBike_001` with its speed, SOC, lane, etc.  
  At timestep 2, the same vehicle appears again, but with updated values (speed increased, energy consumed grew, SOC changed).  

- **That’s why rows repeat the same vehicle ID.**  
  It’s not duplication — it’s a *time series record*. Each row is a new measurement of the same vehicle at a later timestep.

---

## 📊 Example From Your Data

- **Timestep 1, `easyBike_001`**  
  ```
  speed_ms = 0.82, soc_percent = 43.17, energy_consumed_Wh = 0.05
  ```

- **Timestep 2, `easyBike_001`**  
  ```
  speed_ms = 1.40, soc_percent = 43.17, energy_consumed_Wh = 0.16
  ```

Notice:
- The **vehicle ID is the same** (it’s the same EV).  
- The **values changed** (speed and energy consumption updated).  
- SOC stayed constant here because the vehicle hasn’t consumed enough energy yet to drop below 43.17%.  

This shows the dataset is recording the **evolution of each vehicle’s state over time**.

---

## ⚡ Why It Matters

- **For analysis:** You can track how each EV’s SOC decreases while driving, when it enters a charging station, how long it waits, and how much energy it consumes.  
- **For ML forecasting:** This repeated logging creates a panel dataset (time × vehicle), which is perfect for predicting load demand, queue lengths, or SOC depletion.  
- **For system monitoring:** It allows you to aggregate across vehicles at each timestep (system load, average SOC, utilization).

---

## 🎯 Key Takeaway
- **Timesteps = time snapshots.**  
- **Vehicle IDs = unique EVs.**  
- **Rows repeat because the same vehicle is observed at every timestep until it leaves the simulation.**  

So what looks like repetition is actually the **time series tracking of each vehicle’s behavior**.
